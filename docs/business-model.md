# Archimedes business Model

The business model of Archimedes follows the same idea as the platform itself: flexibility.

Businesses have different needs, different sizes, and different ways of operating. Charging everyone for the exact same package would go against the purpose of the project.

The idea is to let each business build its own environment and pay according to what it chooses to use.

## The Basic Idea

A business joins Archimedes, chooses a Sketch that fits its operations, and selects the Modules it wants.

The commercial model is expected to consider three elements:
- **Sketches:** The business environments selected by the customer.
- **Modules:** The additional functionality activated by the customer.
- **Business units:** The number of units using a particular Sketch or Module, when applicable.

This would allow two businesses using Archimedes to have completely different configurations and costs.

## No Forced Bundles

Modules should be optional. A customer shouldn't need to purchase inventory management just to use a CRM, for example. Each module should be independently selectable whenever its functionality allows it.

The same principle applies to Sketches: the goal is to give businesses the environments they need, not lock them into predefined packages.

## Growing With the Business

A business may start with a simple configuration and add more functionality as it grows.

It may activate new modules, expand their use to additional units, or eventually adopt another Sketch if its operations require it.

The commercial model should make this growth possible without making the platform unnecessarily expensive or complicated.

## Custom Solutions

Not every business will fit perfectly into an existing Sketch. When a customer needs something different, Archimedes should have room for custom solutions.

Whenever possible, new functionality should become part of the shared platform rather than creating a completely separate version for one customer. This helps the platform improve as new needs are discovered.

## Subscriptions and Payments

Recurring subscriptions are the initial direction for generating revenue. Payment processing may also create opportunities for additional services and revenue models in the future.

The platform should be able to evolve its billing and payment integrations as its commercial needs become clearer.

## What Still Needs to Be Defined

Several commercial decisions are still open:
- Pricing for each Sketch.
- Pricing for individual Modules.
- How multi-unit usage should be charged.
- Whether businesses using multiple Sketches should receive discounts.
- How custom solutions should be priced.
- Which payment and billing integrations make the most sense.
- Whether additional revenue models should be introduced in the future.

These decisions should be made carefully, considering development costs, platform maintenance, customer needs, and real-world validation.

## Guiding Principle

The business model should support the flexibility of the product, not work against it.

Customers should have room to choose, start small, and expand when they need to.

**The goal is to charge for the value provided, not for features a business never wanted in the first place.**
